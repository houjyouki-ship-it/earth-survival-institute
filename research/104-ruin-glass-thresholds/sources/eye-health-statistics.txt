Even when the original source is known, many statistics are inaccurate or misleading because they rely on self-reports or surveys rather than large, compelling population health studies or meta-analyses. Therefore, it is important to exercise caution when reporting these numbers to patients and the public. Finding Accurate Eye Health Statistics Here are the three best ways to track down the latest and most accurate eye statistics: Reach out to the American Academy of Ophthalmology press office at media@aao.org to request assistance finding information about a specific topic. Visit the Academy's United States eye statistics resource for professionals. On this page, you'll find links to the most comprehensive and accurate datasets produced from national surveys, eye exam-based studies, electronic health records (EHR) and EHR-based clinical data registries, and administrative claims databases. Visit the National Eye Institute's eye statistics page for a list of agencies that collect, analyze and report data on various eye conditions. These agencies may be able to provide the latest and most accurate eye statistics upon request.
