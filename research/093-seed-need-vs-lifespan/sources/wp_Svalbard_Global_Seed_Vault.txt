The '''Svalbard Global Seed Vault''' ({{langx|no|Svalbard globale frøhvelv}}) is a secure backup facility for the [[Earth]]'s [[crop diversity]] on the Norwegian island of [[Spitsbergen]] in the remote [[Arctic]] [[Svalbard]] archipelago.<ref name="Charles2006"/> The Seed Vault provides long-term storage for duplicates of seeds from around the world, conserved in [[gene bank]]s. The Seed Vault is managed under terms spelled out in a tripartite agreement among the Norwegian government, the [[Crop Trust]], and the [[Nordic Genetic Resource Center]] (NordGen).<ref name="whatisnordgen"/><ref name=":3">{{Cite web|title=Svalbard Global Seed Vault|url=https://www.nordgen.org/en/our-work/svalbard-global-seed-vault/|access-date=28 May 2021|website=NordGen|language=en-US}}</ref>

The Norwegian government entirely funded the Seed Vault's approximately {{NOK|45 million}} ({{US$|8.8 million}} in 2008) construction cost.<ref name="nature20080326" /> Norway and the Crop Trust pay for operational costs. Storing seeds in the vault is free to depositors. 

As of June 2025, the Seed Vault conserves 1,355,591 accessions,<ref>{{Cite web|title=Svalbard Global Seed Vault|url=https://seedvault.nordgen.org/Information/Depositors|access-date=2025-06-10|website=seedvault.nordgen.org}}</ref> representing more than 13,000 years of [[History of agriculture|agricultural history]].<ref name="time201704">{{cite magazine|last=Duggan|first=Jennifer|date=April 2017|title=Inside the 'Doomsday' Vault|url=https://time.com/doomsday-vault/|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170408091443/http://time.com/doomsday-vault/|archive-date=8 April 2017|magazine=[[Time (magazine)|Time]]}}</ref> The vault is of very much cultural significance.

==History==
In 1984, the Nordic Gene Bank (now NordGen) began storing backup Nordic plant [[germplasm]] via frozen seeds in an abandoned coal mine outside of [[Longyearbyen]].<ref name=":0">{{Cite web|date=4 July 2017|title=The History|url=https://www.seedvault.no/about/history/|access-date=2 June 2021|website=Svalbard Global Seed Vault|language=en-GB}}</ref>

In 2001, the [[International Treaty on Plant Genetic Resources for Food and Agriculture]] (ITPGRFA) was adopted and national governments began to ratify the treaty soon after. The treaty establishes a multilateral system for plant [[genetic resources]] that includes providing access to the materials and providing mechanisms so that those who use the resources can share any derived benefits.<ref name=":2">{{Cite web|title=Overview {{!}} International Treaty on Plant Genetic Resources for Food and Agriculture|url=http://www.fao.org/plant-treaty/overview/en/|access-date=7 June 2021|website=www.fao.org}}</ref>

A team led by conservationist [[Cary Fowler]] actively campaigned for the development of the Seed Vault and approached the Norwegian Government. Fowler conceptualized the vault, headed the committee that developed the plan for the facility and is the founding chair of the international council that has overseen the vault since its inception.<ref>{{Cite news|last=F|last2=r|last3=e|last4=s|last5=h|last6=A|last7=i|last8=r|date=2017-07-24|title=Inside The Global Seed Vault, Where The History And Future Of Agriculture Is Stored|url=https://www.npr.org/2017/07/24/539005688/inside-the-global-seed-vault-where-the-history-and-future-of-agriculture-is-stor|access-date=2025-06-10|work=NPR|language=en}}</ref> The Norwegian Government gave a contract to the Center for International Environment and Development Studies at the Agricultural University of Norway for Fowler’s role and to the Nordic Gene Bank. Chaired by Fowler, then a professor at the university and a senior advisor in the CGIAR, the committee included Henry Shands (head of the U.S. national gene bank), William George (an engineer), Bent Skovmand (director of the Nordic Gene Bank), and Geoff Hawtin as an observer.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Study to Assess the Feasibility of Establishing a Svalbard Arctic Seed Depository for the International Community|url=https://www.regjeringen.no/globalassets/upload/lmd/kampanjesvalbard/vedlegg/frohvelv_study_to_assess.pdf|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160424201453/https://www.regjeringen.no/globalassets/upload/lmd/kampanjesvalbard/vedlegg/frohvelv_study_to_assess.pdf|archive-date=2016-04-24}}</ref> The team conducted a feasibility study in 2004 and concurred that Svalbard was an appropriate location for long-term storage.<ref>Fowler, C., George, W., Shands, H., Skovmand, B. (2004) [https://www.regjeringen.no/globalassets/upload/lmd/kampanjesvalbard/vedlegg/frohvelv_study_to_assess.pdf Study to Assess the Feasibility of Establishing a Svalbard Arctic Seed Depository for the International Community]. Prepared for the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (Norway). Center for International Environment and Development Studies. Agricultural University of Norway.</ref>

Also in 2004, the ITPGRFA entered into force and provided the legal framework for having one international security facility.<ref name=":2" /> The FAO [[Commission on Genetic Resources for Food and Agriculture]] endorsed the initiative<ref>{{Cite web|date=8–12 November 2004|title=Tenth Regular Session of the Commission on Genetic Resources for Food and Agriculture|url=http://www.fao.org/3/j3951e/j3951e.pdf|access-date=7 June 2021|website=FAO}}</ref> and in October 2004 the Norwegian Government committed to fund the Seed Vault and begin the construction.<ref name=":0" /> In 2006, Geoffrey Hawtin was appointed to prepare a report on technical, administrative and political issues.<ref>{{Cite web|last=|first=|date=2024|title=Svalbard Global Seed Vault – Theme for World Food Prize 2024|url=https://www.seedvault.no/2024/05/10/svalbard-global-seed-vault-theme-for-world-food-prize-2024/|access-date=2024-05-14|website=Svalbard Global Seed Vault|language=en-US}}</ref>

The Seed Vault officially opened on 26 February 2008,<ref name="Mellgren2008" /> although the first seeds arrived in January 2008.<ref name="Kinver2008" />

As part of the Seed Vault's first anniversary, more than 90,000 food crop seed samples were placed into storage, bringing the total number of seed samples to 400,000.<ref name="time20090227" /> Among the new seeds included were 32 varieties of potatoes from [[Republic of Ireland|Ireland]]'s national genebanks and 20,000 new samples from the U.S. [[Agricultural Research Service]].<ref name="HPJ2009" /> Other seed samples came from genebanks in [[Canada]] and [[Switzerland]] as well as international genebanks in [[Colombia]], [[Mexico]] and [[Syria]].<ref name="Young2009" /> This {{cvt|4|t|adj=on}} shipment brought the total number of seeds stored in the Seed Vault to over 200 million.<ref name="time20090227" /> As of this anniversary, the Seed Vault contained samples from approximately one-third of the world's most important food crop varieties.<ref name="Young2009" /> Also as part of the anniversary, experts on [[Effects of climate change on agriculture|food production and climate change]] met for a three-day conference in [[Longyearbyen]].<ref name="bbc20090226" />

Japanese sculptor {{ill|Mitsuaki Tanabe|ja|田辺光彰}} presented a work to the Seed Vault named "The Seed 2009 / Momi In-Situ Conservation".<ref name="norwayjp2009"/>

In 2010, a delegation of seven [[United States Senate|U.S. senators]] deposited a number of different varieties of [[chili pepper]].<ref name="bbc20100713" />

By 2013, approximately one-third of the [[genus|genera]] diversity stored in genebanks globally was represented at the Seed Vault.<ref name="Westengen2013"/>

In 2015, researchers started sending seeds from the Middle East<ref>{{Cite web|last=Kloosterman|first=Karin|date=2023-03-13|title=Seed banks in the Middle East – Green Prophet|url=https://www.greenprophet.com/2023/03/seed-banks-in-the-middle-east/|access-date=2023-09-26|language=en-US}}</ref> for safeguarding in Svalbard due to ongoing conflicts.

In October 2016, the Seed Vault experienced an unusually large degree of water intrusion due to higher than average temperatures and heavy rainfall. While it is common for some water to seep into the Seed Vault's {{cvt|100|m|ft|0|adj=on}} entrance tunnel during the warmer spring months, in this case the water encroached {{cvt|15|m|ft}} into the tunnel before freezing.<ref name="popsci20170522"/> Because the Seed Vault was designed to be able to handle water intrusion, the seeds were not at risk.<ref name="popsci20170522" /> As a result, however, the Norwegian public works agency [[Statsbygg]] completed improvements to the tunnel in 2019 to prevent any such intrusion in the future, including waterproofing the tunnel walls, removing heat sources from the tunnel, and digging exterior drainage ditches.<ref name="statsbygg20170520"/><ref name=":1">{{Cite web|title=Statsbygg: Svalbard Global Seed Vault|url=https://www.statsbygg.no/prosjekter-og-eiendommer/svalbard-globale-frohvelv|access-date=28 May 2021|website=www.statsbygg.no}}</ref>

For the Seed Vault's 10th anniversary on 26 February 2018, a shipment of 70,000 samples was delivered to the facility, bringing the number of samples received to more than one million (not counting withdrawals).<ref name="newfood20180226">{{cite news|url=https://www.newfoodmagazine.com/news/65063/seed-vault-marks-10th-anniversary/|title=Seed Vault marks 10th anniversary with 70,000 new samples|work=New Food|first=George|last=Smith|date=26 February 2018|access-date=22 October 2018}}</ref>

According to ''[[The Independent]]'' the [[COVID-19 pandemic]] did not pose a risk to the vault "as there are no permanent staff at the Svalbard facility."<ref>{{Cite web|last=Boyl|first=Louise|date=2020-03-27|title=The 'Doomsday' seed vault protecting the world's crops amid catastrophes like coronavirus|url=https://www.independent.co.uk/climate-change/news/doomsday-seed-vault-coronavirus-svalbard-norway-food-supplies-a9431031.html|archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/archive/20220525/https://web.archive.org/web/20210918012101/https://www.independent.co.uk/climate-change/news/doomsday-seed-vault-coronavirus-svalbard-norway-food-supplies-a9431031.html|archive-date=18 September 2021|url-access=subscription|url-status=live|access-date=2021-09-18|website=[[The Independent]]|language=en}}</ref>

In 2019, the Seed Vault cost about {{NOK|2.4 million}} (US$282,000) to maintain.<ref name="Annual2019">{{cite web|url=https://www.nordgen.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/SeedVault_Annual-ProgressReport2019_JuneRevision.pdf|title=Svalbard Global Seed Vault: Annual Progress Report 2019|publisher=Nordic Genetic Resource Center|date=June 2020}}</ref>

==Construction==
[[File:Cary Fowler 2007.jpg|thumb|left|[[Cary Fowler]] at the Seed Vault during its construction]]

[[Prime Minister of Norway|Norway]], [[Prime Minister of Sweden|Sweden]], [[Prime Minister of Finland|Finland]], [[Prime Minister of Denmark|Denmark]], and [[Prime Minister of Iceland|Iceland]]'s [[prime minister]]s ceremonially laid "the first stone" on 19 June 2006.<ref name="bbc20060619"/>

The [[seedbank|seed bank]] is {{cvt|130|m}} inside a [[sandstone]] mountain on Spitsbergen Island,<ref name="hist"/> and employs robust security systems. The facility is managed by the [[Nordic Genetic Resource Center]], though there are no permanent staff on-site.

Spitsbergen was considered ideal because it lacked [[Plate tectonics|tectonic activity]] and had [[permafrost]], which aids preservation. It being {{cvt|130|m}} above [[sea level]] will keep the site dry even if the [[ice cap]]s melt.<ref name="hist"/> Locally mined coal provides power for refrigeration units that further cool the seeds to the internationally recommended standard of {{cvt|-18|C|F|1}}.<ref name="regjeringen"/> If the equipment fails, at least several weeks will elapse before the facility rises to the surrounding sandstone bedrock's temperature of {{cvt|-3|C|F}},<ref name="Charles2006" /> and is estimated to take two centuries to warm to {{cvt|0|C|F}}.<ref name="economist2012"/>

A feasibility study prior to construction determined that the Seed Vault could preserve most major food crops' seeds for hundreds of years. Some, including those of important grains, could potentially remain viable for thousands of years.<ref name="Fowler2008"/>

Running the length of the facility's roof and down the front face to the entryway is an illuminated artwork named ''Perpetual Repercussion'' by Norwegian artist Dyveke Sanne that marks the location of the vault from a distance.<ref name="pubartnorway"/> In Norway, government-funded construction projects exceeding a certain cost must include artwork. [[:no:Kunst i offentlige rom|KORO]], the Norwegian State agency overseeing art in public spaces, engaged the artist to propose an artwork for the Seed Vault. The roof and vault entrance are filled with highly reflective stainless steel, mirrors, and prisms. The installation reflects polar light in the summer months, while in the winter, a network of 200 [[fibre-optic cable]]s gives the piece a muted greenish-turquoise and white light.<ref name="croptrust-lighting"/>

==Mission==
The Seed Vault's mission is to provide a backup against accidental loss of diversity in traditional genebanks. While the popular press has emphasized its possible utility in the event of a major regional or global catastrophe, the Seed Vault will be more frequently accessed when genebanks lose samples due to mismanagement, accident, equipment failures, funding cuts, and [[natural disaster]]s. These events occur with some regularity. War and civil strife have a history of destroying some genebanks. The national genebank of the Philippines was damaged by flooding and later destroyed by a fire, the genebanks of Afghanistan and Iraq have been lost completely, while an international genebank in Syria became unavailable.<ref name="economist2012"/> According to ''[[The Economist]]'', "the Svalbard vault is a backup for the world's 1,750 seed banks, storehouses of [[crop diversity|agricultural biodiversity]]."<ref name="economist2012"/>

Norwegian law has prohibited the storing of genetically modified seeds at the vault.<ref name="sgsv-faq" />

The adjacent [[Arctic World Archive]] provides a similar service for data, which is etched as code into reels of film. Project lead Piql of Norway states that the film, when properly preserved, should last for 1,000 years.<ref name="livesci20170401"/>

== Tripartite agreement ==
The Seed Vault is managed under terms spelled out in a tripartite agreement among the Norwegian Government, the Crop Trust, and the Nordic Genetic Resource Center (NordGen).<ref name="whatisnordgen" /><ref name=":3" /> The Kingdom of Norway owns the Seed Vault. The Crop Trust provides funding for ongoing operations and provides financial assistance to depositors in their preparation of shipments. NordGen operates the Seed Vault and maintains the public database of the deposits.<ref>{{Cite web|title=FAQ about the Seed Vault|url=https://www.croptrust.org/our-work/svalbard-global-seed-vault/faq-about-the-vault/|access-date=7 June 2021|website=Crop Trust|language=en-US}}</ref>

An International Advisory Council provides guidance and advice. It includes representatives from the [[Food and Agriculture Organization|FAO]], [[CGIAR]], the International Treaty on Plant Genetic Resources and other institutions.<ref name="jia2014">{{cite journal|last=Fowler|first=Cary|date=Spring–Summer 2014|title=A 'Doomsday' Seed Vault to Protect the World's Diversity – An interview with Cary Fowler|url=https://jia.sipa.columbia.edu/files/2014/10/141-146_Fowler_Feature.pdf|journal=Journal of International Affairs|volume=67|issue=2|pages=141–146|jstor=24461740|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141209012746/https://jia.sipa.columbia.edu/files/2014/10/141-146_Fowler_Feature.pdf|archive-date=9 December 2014}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|title=International Advisory Panel|url=https://www.nordgen.org/en/our-work/svalbard-global-seed-vault/international-advisory-panel/|access-date=28 May 2021|website=NordGen|language=en-US}}</ref>

==Access to seeds==
[[File:Storage containers in Svalbard Global Seed Vault 01.jpg|thumb|upright|Seed storage containers on metal shelving inside the vault]]

Vault seed samples are copies of samples stored in the depositing genebanks. Researchers, plant breeders, and other groups wishing to access seed samples cannot do so through the Seed Vault; they must instead request samples from the depositing genebanks. The samples stored in the genebanks will, in most cases, be accessible in accordance with the terms and conditions of the [[International Treaty on Plant Genetic Resources for Food and Agriculture]], approved by 148 countries or parties.<ref name="Fowler2008"/><ref>{{Cite web|title=Contracting Parties: International Treaty on Plant Genetic Resources for Food and Agriculture|publisher=Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations|url=http://www.fao.org/plant-treaty/countries/membership/en/|access-date=20 May 2021}}</ref>

The Seed Vault functions like a safe deposit box in a bank. The bank owns the building and the depositor owns the contents of their box. The Government of Norway owns the facility and the depositing genebanks own the seeds they send. The deposit of samples in Svalbard does not constitute a legal transfer of genetic resources. In genebank terminology this is called a "black box" arrangement. Each depositor signs a Deposit Agreement with NordGen, acting on behalf of [[Norway]]. The Agreement makes clear that Norway does not claim ownership over the deposited samples and that ownership remains with the depositor, who has the sole right of access to those materials in the seed vault. No one has access to anyone else's seeds from the seed vault.<ref name="Fowler2008" /><ref name="regjeringen-mo"/> The database of samples and depositors is maintained by NordGen.<ref name="nordgendatabase"/>

==Seed storage==

The seeds are stored in sealed three-ply foil packages and then placed into plastic tote containers on metal shelving racks.<ref name="croptrust"/> The storage rooms are kept at {{cvt|-18|C|F|1}}. The low temperature and limited access to oxygen will ensure low metabolic activity and delay seed ageing. The permafrost surrounding the facility will help maintain the low temperature of the seeds if the electricity supply fails.<ref name="regjeringen-faq" /><ref name="regjeringen-mo" />

Initially the Seed Vault would have some minor water intrusion at its entrance during the annual spring permafrost thawing. Warmer temperatures and heavy rainfall in October 2016 caused significantly greater amounts of water to seep into the entrance, but the facility's design ensured that the water froze after several meters and the seeds were not endangered.<ref name="popsci20170522" /><ref name="cnn20170521" /> Work completed in 2019 eliminated this water seepage.<ref name=":1" />

Attached to the seed boxes are sheets of nanofilm that hold information on such things as seed identity.<ref>{{Cite web|date=2022-02-14|title=Nanofilm will safeguard identity of the more than 1 mill seed samples in the Seed Vault|url=https://www.seedvault.no/news/nanofilm-will-safeguard-identity-of-the-more-than-1-mill-seed-samples-in-the-seed-vault/|access-date=2022-03-04|website=Svalbard Global Seed Vault|language=en-GB}}</ref>

==Crop Trust==
The [[Crop Trust]], officially known as the Global Crop Diversity Trust, plays a key role in the planning of the Seed Vault and coordinating shipments of seed samples to the Seed Vault in conjunction with the Nordic Genetic Resource Center. The Crop Trust provides most of the annual operating costs for the facility and has set aside an endowment fund to do so,<ref>{{Cite web|title=The Endowment Fund|url=https://www.croptrust.org/our-mission/crop-diversity-endowment-fund/|access-date=28 May 2021|website=Crop Trust|language=en-US}}</ref> while the Norwegian government finances upkeep of the structure itself. With support of its donors, the Crop Trust assists selected genebanks in developing countries as well as the international agricultural research centres in packaging and shipping seeds to the Seed Vault.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Saving the World, One Seed at a Time|url=https://www.croptrust.org/news-events/opinions/saving-the-world-one-seed-at-a-time/|access-date=2024-05-14|website=www.croptrust.org|language=en}}</ref>

=== Indigenous communities ===
Depositors to the Seed Vault are not limited to international, regional and national genebanks. Some indigenous communities have deposited seeds for safety duplication in the Seed Vault. In 2015, representatives of the Parque de la Papa in Peru deposited 750 samples of potatoes.<ref>{{Cite web|date=27 August 2015|title=Andean Potato Varieties Lost, Now Safe Forever|url=https://www.croptrust.org/press-release/andean-potato-varieties-lost-now-safeguarded-forever/|access-date=16 June 2021|website=Crop Trust|language=en-US}}</ref> In 2020, the [[Cherokee Nation]] became the first US tribe to deposit when it safeguarded nine samples of heirloom food crops which predate European colonization.<ref>{{Cite web|last=GrrlScientist|title=Cherokee Nation Sends Heirloom Seeds To 'Doomsday' Seed Vault In Norway|url=https://www.forbes.com/sites/grrlscientist/2020/02/18/cherokee-nation-sends-heirloom-seeds-to-doomsday-seed-vault-in-norway/|access-date=16 June 2021|website=Forbes|language=en}}</ref>

==Cultural importance==
The seed vault is a common reference in different forms of fiction and media, often as an example of international collaboration, similar to the [[International Space Station]], as a media symbol for the potential of [[Global catastrophic risk|doomsday scenarios]], and a point of conversation about the sustainability of human society.<ref name=":4">{{Cite web|last=Martinussen|first=Einar S.|date=2017-07-08|title=Cultural expressions of the Seed Vault|url=https://medium.com/explorations-of-the-seed-vault/cultural-expressions-of-the-seed-vault-62a412d3efc|access-date=2024-07-13|website=Explorations of the Seed Vault|language=en}}</ref> Even before the Seed Vault officially opened, there was a feature article in The New Yorker.<ref>{{Cite news|last=Seabrook|first=John|date=2007-08-20|title=Sowing for Apocalypse|url=https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2007/08/27/sowing-for-apocalypse|access-date=2025-06-10|work=The New Yorker|language=en-US|issn=0028-792X}}</ref>  Science communicators have been important in taking the project from relative obscurity, to global awareness.<ref name=":4" /> For example, Cary Fowler gave a TED talk on the Seed Vault at Oxford in 2009.<ref>{{Cite AV media|url=https://www.ted.com/talks/cary_fowler_one_seed_at_a_time_protecting_the_future_of_food|title=One seed at a time, protecting the future of food|date=2009-08-31|last=Fowler|first=Cary|language=en|access-date=2025-06-10|via=www.ted.com}}</ref> 

The Seed Vault was the inspiration for Ibsen International's art project "The Seed", supported by the Norwegian government. The children's opera ''[[Children of Ginko]]'' ({{langx|no|Frøbarna}}) which aimed to raise ecological awareness, "reveal the power of nature and celebrate children's courage in growing up",<ref name="Ibsen">{{cite web|url=https://ibseninternational.com/productions/children-of-ginko-premiere-in-shanghai/|title=Children of Ginko – Premiere in Shanghai|publisher=Ibsen International|access-date=23 December 2020}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.klassiskmusikk.com/nyheter/marcus-paus-premiere-i-shanghai/|title=Marcus Paus-premiere i Shanghai|website=Klassisk musikk|access-date=23 December 2020}}</ref> was created as part of this project. 

There are several children’s books about the seed vault, including The Garden at the End of the World, and Just in Case: Saving Seeds in the Svalbard Global Seed Vault.<ref>{{Cite web|date=2024-05-01|title=The Garden at the End of the World by Cassy Polimeni and Briony Stewart|url=https://paperbarkwords.blog/2024/05/01/the-garden-at-the-end-of-the-world-by-cassy-polimeni-and-briony-stewart/|access-date=2025-06-10|language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|title=Just in Case: Saving Seeds in the Svalbard Global Seed Vault (Hardcover)|url=https://www.brilliant-books.net/book/9781623544805|access-date=2025-06-10|website=Brilliant Books}}</ref> The seed vault has  also been the subject of two feature length documentaries: Seeds of Time and Seed Battles, as well as Forever Securing the World Food Supply.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Seeds of Time|url=https://www.seedsoftimemovie.com/|access-date=2025-06-10|website=Seeds of Time|language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite AV media|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=scP4L4bOPA8|title=Seeds battles - The Doomsday vault - VPRO documentary - 2013|date=2017-05-21|last=vpro documentary|access-date=2025-06-10|via=YouTube}}</ref>

In 2011, Norway issued a postage stamp to honor the seed vault.<ref>{{Citation|last=Trust|first=Crop|title=Svalbard Global Seed Vault stamp in Norway|date=2011-04-14|url=https://www.flickr.com/photos/croptrust/5621477154/|access-date=2025-06-10}}</ref>
