                               THE HISTORY OF CARVER COUNTY

        The Carver County Historical Society is a private, not-for-profit 501(C)3 organization serving the
residents of Carver County and beyond. We are located across the street from Bayview Elementary
School in downtown Waconia. We have five affiliate groups: Chaska Historical Society, Chanhassen
Historical Society, Wilkommen Heritage and Preservation Society of Norwood Young America, the
Watertown Area Historical Society and the Waconia Heritage Association. In 2022, it celebrated its
82nd anniversary. The Historic Andrew Peterson Farmstead was added in 2015.

                                              As we roll into 2023, I’d like to start by thanking our many
                                           members who contribute every year to the Carver County Historical
                                           Society. Your contributions enable the CCHS to operate, grow, and
                                           continue to offer the many programs and resources that are available to
                                           members!

                                           I’d also like to thank Wendy and her staff for all they have done
                     throughout the past year in operating the CCHS through these uncertain times and through
                     continued pandemic conditions. The CCHS is seeing some improvement in attendance
                     numbers, but attendance is still down compared to pre-covid numbers.

                     At the end of 2021, long-time Librarian Marilyn Braun retired, and at the beginning of 2022
                     we hired Jayna Hulleman and Brenda Reister to our staff.
President’s Report




                     The year 2022 was very challenging for the CCHS staff and board members as we continued
                     our pledge drive fundraiser for the Historic Andrew Peterson farm. The CCHS was able to
                     participate in the Jeffris Family Foundation challenge program and had a $500,000 goal to
                     achieve to receive the $250,000.00 from the Jeffris Foundation. With help from our many
                     donors and help from Carver County, we made it! In three years, we managed to raise
                     $1,051,025 – with $750,000.00 designated to the historic farmhouse. The balance will be
                     used on the other buildings and property needs. The middle 1914 dairy barn will be trans-
                     formed into an event and interpretive center. Sidenote: the CCHS was the ONLY organiza-
                     tion of 5 organizations to complete the Jeffris challenge and meet the fundraising goal!

                     With the fundraising done for now, work has begun at the farm! The main project is the
                     farmhouse, and the “newer” porches have been removed, any modern construction has been
                     removed, and general restoration has begun in the house. Many layers of wallpaper were
                     found, original steps were found under the visible steps (because the original was too steep),
                     and animal hair was found in the original plaster and lathe – a technique mentioned in An-
                     drew Peterson’s diary! We also found the original log structure, which makes up the core of
                     the house in great condition. In the next few years, it will be exciting to see the progress on
                     the house and other structures at the farm.

                     Let’s hear it for Wendy!! – she has done it all in the past few years – from managing the
                     CCHS, to managing a major fund drive, managing changes in staffing, Covid challenges, she
                     has had her hands full! Thanks Wendy, for all you do!

                     Sincerely,

                     Dan Lund
                     Carver County Historical Society
                     Board of Directors President

                                                                     Welcome to 2022 in review and what a 2022 it was!
                                                            In past years and in much of my writings, I start with a quote. This year, it
                                                        was impossible to find one that represented 2022 accurately. How can you
                                                        accurately express the rollercoaster of emotion that occurred while trying to
                                                        complete a $750,000 challenge capital campaign- during Covid shutdowns?
                                                        There was also the Covid recovery which while making progress, we are still
                                                        behind previous visitation numbers. We also lost a much-beloved employee to
                                                        retirement.
                                                          The beginning of 2022 marked two very challenging events. The first is that
                                                          we lost Marilyn Braun to retirement. Her knowledge of the library and the
                                                          families in Carver County made for some very large shoes to fill. Marilyn was
                                                          and still is instrumental in organizing and making sure the newspaper indexing
                              is up to date-and accurate. This is not a small job as there are well over 300,000 index cards that
                              document the information in the Carver County newspapers. The CCHS wishes Marilyn all the best in her
                              retirement, but we all know you can’t hold a good person down for long. Marilyn will still be seen in the
Executive Director’s Report




                              library as she continues as a volunteer, working alongside our committed library volunteers.
                              At the beginning of 2022, we had raised $320,094 toward the Jeffris Family Foundation capital campaign.
                              We had six months to go and needed $179,906 to raise to reach our $500K goal. If we failed, we would
                              not receive anything from the Jeffris Foundation. Our Fundraising Committee agreed that win or lose, we
                              would give it our all until we ran out of time.
                              Several of us had seen Covid funds floating around, and we discussed options about how we might tap
                              into those. A letter arrived from a foundation that made it clear that they couldn't help as they had
                              changed their giving criteria to giving to food shelves. It was the ammunition we needed. The CCHS
                              had also received Covid funds due to our mandatory shutdown, giving us the flexibility to request the
                              County designate our Q1 appropriation to the Jeffris campaign. I made the call to the County and made
                              the sales pitch. It wouldn't cost the County anything to designate our quarterly appropriation to the
                              campaign, as it was already in the budget. Then the fundraising committee voted to go one step fur-
                              ther. We felt we could raise an additional $40,000, so we would ask for an additional $85,000 using the
                              letter we had received that stated the change in giving priorities due to Covid. This made the argument
                              that Covid had indeed horribly affected our campaign. A date was set for us to present in front of the
                              Carver County Board of Commissioners.
                              Before the day arrived for the presentation, we struggled to dig for the remaining $40,000. Several
                              donations came in dropping the amount needed to $18,000. A donor called and said he would donate
                              $14,000 IF we could raise the other $4,000. A check for $3000 came in then a check for $650 and finally
                              a $400 check and the donor’s $14,000. Now, if only we could get the requested amount from the County.
                              The day came. The majority of our CCHS board showed up. More people showed up remotely, including
                              our friends in Sweden. Letters of support came in. I presented. A motion was made to approve the $85K
                              appropriation. All commissioners agreed, THEN, Commissioner Udermann motioned to appropriate
                              $500,000. Commissioners Udermann, Fahey, and Workman voted in favor of the $500,000 with Lynch
                              and Degler against, noting that they supported the CCHS, but felt the larger amount should follow the
                              proper procedure. Motion passed. We had the funds we needed to complete the campaign and then
                              some. I was in shock.
                              The $500,000 campaign goal was verified by the Jeffris Family Foundation and a check for $250,000
                              arrived via certified mail. I held it and looked at it for a long time before depositing it.
                              Four other organizations started a capital campaign at the same time we did with the Jeffris
                              Foundation. We were the only organization to succeed. Construction on the house started in the fall of
                              2022, almost exactly 10 years after receiving the property
                              Thank you to everyone for your supporter! Without you, it would not be possible to achieve all we have.
                              Wendy Petersen Biorn

                                                                   Hello everyone!


                                       The year 2022 has finally come to an end. With the extraordinary amount of snow that
                                       has already fallen in December, I think everyone was ready to finish up the year and
                                       look forward to an early spring.
                                       My position at the CCHS is working with accounting, gift shop inventory, and
                                       membership renewals and databases.
                                         We are moving forward with a number of streamlining processes that will make my job
                                         easier and more accurate. Those include paying many of our Accounts Payable vendors
                 electronically. This reduces the time and cost of cutting checks and mailing. CCHS also moved to a different
                 payroll service which significantly reduced our costs. We now have immediate access to the reports we use to
                 record the payroll in our accounting software. More importantly, we lost no support by making the change.
                 Access to W2s and year-end reports is easier and faster as well.
                 During the summer, Wendy and I worked with Peterson Company, Ltd, our accounting firm, to complete an
                 internal audit. These are required by our by-laws every five years. Since most of our records are available in
Office Manager




                 our Quickbooks online account, the accounting firm was able to access our records and complete most of the
                 tasks remotely. When they requested information regarding certain accounts with hardcopy files, we were able
                 to access and provide the reports relatively easily, electronically. The audit was completed with a positive
                 review in early Fall.
                 We completed the Jeffris Challenge Campaign this year. Funds will be used to rehabilitate Andrew Peterson’s
                 farmhouse. It was so exciting to see that come together. Now that the work on the farmhouse has begun, it is
                 so interesting to see wallpaper and linoleum that date back so many years – for me, to my grandmother’s time.
                 It seems like the workers found new, interesting items weekly, as the work progressed.
                 In late November, we began our Annual Fund Drive and had a good response. We are so grateful for your
                 support. The end of the year has been busy getting thank you letters out to our members and donors and
                 wrapping up the year-end
                 accounting tasks.

                                                          the Borchardt Log Granary on the Carver County Fairgrounds. Artisan
                                                          Restoration from Kasota, MN completed the project, which included
                                                          lifting, full & partial replacement of several logs, new chinking, and a

                                                          Norwood Young America Lions Club; Athwin Foundation; and the
                                                          Clarence Kelzer Family.

                         benefitted from an unexpected donation to the Collections Care budget from a CCHS member. The
                         donation paid for additional archival sleeves for protecting documents & photographs, tools for the
                         processing and care of artifacts, and an overhead camera/scanner for digitizing books and over-sized

                                                         My name is Brenda Reister and joined the CCHS staff in March
                                                      upon Marilyn Braun’s retirement. It’s been an exciting first (almost)
                                                      year and no small task trying to follow Marilyn! Her knowledge,
                                                      experience, and passion for the library, visitors and knowledge of
                                                      Carver County history are hard to follow. I’m excited to be on staff,
                                                      and look forward to learning more, and assisting researchers.
                                                        There were some changes with library volunteer staff. One long-time
                      volunteer left to take a paid position, another retired this November while two joined us this fall.
                      Most volunteers are back ‘in person’, as opposed to working from home as was done during the worst
                      of Covid. We currently have 10 active volunteers. It has been obvious to me from the start how
                      valuable the volunteers are to the library’s success! What a pool of knowledgeable, devoted
                      individuals who donate their time to build our database and resources ensuring complete and accurate
                      data for researchers.
                      It’s been so interesting to learn how the library is used. I’m becoming more familiar with our
                      resources and am able to fill requests more quickly, and with fewer questions for other staff
                      members. One interesting client was from Windsor, England and was researching an ancestor who
Researcher’s Report




                      was vital to the establishment of the cemetery in Fort Snelling. She was so pleased that we were able
                      to provide her with specifics from obituaries and our photo archives. Another client was doing
                      research for a news story that aired on ABC’s “20/20” program. Requests continue to come in from
                      all over the U.S. and it’s rewarding to be able to help people find pieces of their family puzzle.
                      We’re continuing the index card project that was started in summer of 2020, with the goal of
                      reducing the number of duplicate cards stored, by consolidating information. And, at the same time,
                      any missing data is researched and added to all appropriate cards. The final step is editing the online
                      database to reflect these additions and corrections.
                      I am excited to announce that the Minnesota Historical Society is digitizing our Carver County
                      newspapers between 2013 to 2021. After they finish the digitizing, they will produce microfilm
                      which will be added to our microfilm resource base for visitors.
                      Another project in the works is the updating the library resource inventory.
                      Thank you for making me feel welcomed and I’m looking forward to a great 2023!
                      Brenda Reister
