Executive Director’s Message
This year marks important milestones in our history: the 25th anniversary of the NNMCC at Nikkei Place, which
opened on September 22, 2000, and the 50th anniversary of the founding of our society, the Japanese Canadian
Society of Greater Vancouver for Community Facilities, established on September 16, 1975. Our path—shaped by
decades of collective effort, vision, and resilience—continues to unfold, with each of us contributing to its journey.

Membership grew to over 700+ —an encouraging sign of deepening connection and engagement. Our dedicated
volunteers remain essential to all aspects of our work and their steadfast commitment is a powerful reminder of
the heartfelt spirit that reflects our community.

The Building Committee made significant progress on much-needed upgrades to our 25 year old facility to
keep it safe, functional, and welcoming for years to come. Major improvements to the roof, heating and cooling
systems are underway and on track for completion this summer. As we prepare to launch the Nikkei Next Capital
Campaign in 2025, we look forward to sharing new opportunities to be a part of ongoing improvements that
will sustain a vibrant Centre for the next 25 years and beyond.

The Sakura-Momiji Project continued to advance thanks to the vision and support of Ken Shinozaki and dedicated
efforts of Keiko Funahashi (Nikkei Place Foundation), Henry Wakabayashi, Nick Sueyoshi, and the Vancouver
Japanese Gardeners Association. You will see a new garden, sakura and momiji trees along Kushiro Lane and
Magnolia Lane, surrounding Nikkei Place in vibrant growth and symbolism.

Meaningful progress was also made on our Heritage Hub project, undertaken through the JCLS Heritage
Preservation Pillar. We approach this initiative with humility bearing the responsibility to ensure a lasting impact.
While we are based in British Columbia, this project holds national significance and global reach, and we look
forward to sharing more with the community in the coming year.

I’m continually inspired by the dedication and care our staff bring to their work. We celebrate three long-serving
team members whose contributions have helped shape who we are today:

•   Nichola Ogiwara, who joined in 2004, has brought 20 years of creativity, clarity of purpose, and dedication
    to numerous roles, and remains a steady and trusted presence as Communications Coordinator.
•   Ella Law, with us since 2009, is a vital part-time team member for 15 years whose accounting expertise and
    integrity support both NNMCC and NPF with exceptional care and precision.
•   Patrick Fujisawa, who began as a volunteer and joined staff 10 years ago, serves as Facility Team Coordinator,
    where his hard work and dedication ensure the smooth running of events, rentals, and day-to-day activities.

Their contributions are a testament to the passion and purpose that drive our work every day.

To all who walk this path with us—members, volunteers, staff, donors, partners, and community—thank you. Your
continued support guides our way forward.




Karah Goshinmon Foster, Executive Director

President’s Report
As President of the Nikkei National Museum & Cultural Centre, I have the honour of once again addressing
this annual President’s Report to you, our valued members.

On September 22nd, we will celebrate the 25th year anniversary of the opening of the Centre. We have much
to be proud of! Thanks to the generosity of our Community, the expertise and dedication of our staff, and the
enthusiastic support of our many volunteers, the Centre remains as vibrant as ever. Our thought-provoking
Museum exhibits and our engaging cultural programming have uniquely positioned us to continue with our
mission to honour, preserve, and share the history and heritage of Japanese Canadians and Japanese culture
in Canada.

As we approach our silver anniversary, it is inevitable that we have had to mark the passing of many notable
members of our Community. Without diminishing the accomplishments or importance of others who have
sadly passed away during the past year, I feel compelled to mention Kaye Kaminishi, who, until his passing on
September 28, 2024, was the last surviving member of the storied Vancouver Asahi baseball team, and Grace
Eiko Thomson, our inaugural museum executive director and curator, who sadly passed away on July 11, 2024.

Kaye was a revered ambassador for our Community, known for his dignity and humility. He was featured in
Historica Canada’s 2019 Heritage Minutes vignette – produced with support from the Nikkei National Museum
- honouring the team and its role in helping to briefly transcend some of the racial barriers that were faced
by our Community in the decade before the Second World War, at least amongst fans of the team who were
drawn from all walks of life. Grace was a passionate leader of our Community who was instrumental not only in
keeping the Japanese Canadian wartime experience in the public eye, but also in raising current awareness of
the Vancouver Asahi – as personified by Kaye – and the team’s impact that extended well beyond the baseball
field. Together Kaye and Grace memorialized the team beginning with her seminal exhibition Leveling the
Playing Field in 2002.

It was an honour for me to be invited to once again participate as your President in the most recent
Conference of Heads of Japanese Missions in Canada, an annual meeting of Consuls General of Japan in
Canada hosted by His Excellency, Ambassador Yamanouchi, at the Embassy of Japan in Ottawa, in early
December 2024. The Conference afforded me with the opportunity to reconnect with my counterparts from
across Canada, and to provide Ambassador Yamanouchi, the Consuls General, and representatives of the
Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Japan, with an update on our Centre’s activities. It was also delightful to catch
up with Deputy Head of Mission Maruyama, Consul General Takahashi’s immediate predecessor in Vancouver,
who made time to meet with me despite the demands of his busy schedule.

On February 14, 2025, we signed a Memorandum of Understanding with Mitsubishi Canada Ltd., marking
the beginning of a three-year partnership that will enhance and expand our TAIKEN Field Trips Program.
Each TAIKEN Field Trip offers an immersive educational experience that brings Japanese Canadian history
and culture to life for participating students, exploring key moments from early immigration to the internment
era and beyond. This partnership will allow more students to participate in enhanced activities supported by
expanded educational resources, leading to an improved field trip experience. We wish to thank Mitsubishi
Canada Ltd. for their dedication to education and cultural understanding. We also wish to acknowledge and
thank Mr. Kaori (Mick) Namiki who, until he recently wrapped up his tenure as President and Chief Executive
Officer of Mitsubishi Canada Ltd., served on our Board of Directors for the past five years.

As I mentioned in my last annual report, we have embarked upon an ambitious multi-year project to
implement a Digital Database Hub and Heritage Portal, which is being funded through B.C. Redress under the
auspices of the Japanese Canadian Legacies Society. The project is being facilitated by RYŌSHIN Solutions,
a local project management firm with a proven track record and deep connections to our Community, whose
team reports directly to our Executive Director, Karah Foster. The project’s direction has drawn heavily on
our Museum Staff’s expertise in digitization and curation of archival materials and has been informed in part
by the National Japanese Canadian Digitization Strategy.[1] We also wish to acknowledge and thank Canon
Canada Inc. for offering certain technical services in support of this project.

Sherri Kajiwara, Director-Curator of the Nikkei National Museum, with the support of her skilled and dedicated
team, has continued to work hard to enhance the Museum’s profile and well-earned reputation not only in
Canada but also abroad – most recently in Peru, where she and Dr. Jordan Stanger-Ross were invited to
speak on the topic of Connecting Nikkei Communities through Past Wrongs, Future Choices at the 2nd Nikkei
Museums & Archives Symposium. Sherri continues to serve as Chair of the Museum Exhibit Cluster, and Lisa
Uyeda, Collections Manager continues to serve as co-chair of the Archives Cluster of Past Wrongs, Future
Choices, the multi-year, multinational research and educational project that I referenced in my report last year.
Past Wrongs, Future Choices follows on the success of the University of Victoria-led Landscapes of Injustice
project in which the Centre was a significant partner.

Throughout the past year, the Centre has remained a focal point for Japanese culture in the Lower Mainland.
Favourites such as the Spring Bazaar, Nikkei Craft Fair, and Mochitsuki continue to attract enthusiastic and
diverse patrons, and the 13th annual Nikkei Matsuri Festival will once again bring excitement to Nikkei Place
with one of Canada’s largest authentic and family-friendly Japanese festivals.

Governance
Our current Board of Directors held its first meeting immediately following our last annual general meeting
and has continued to meet bi-monthly. The Executive Committee has continued to meet bi-monthly.
Meetings of the Finance Committee, the Fundraising Committee, the Governance Committee and the
Communications Committee have been held on an as-required basis.

Acknowledgement and Appreciation
We continue to enjoy a warm and supportive relationship with all levels of Government. We have continued to
welcome local Members of Parliament and Members of the Legislative Assembly to many of our events, and
the Honourable David Eby, Premier of the Province of British Columbia, was a special guest at the opening
ceremony of our 12th annual Nikkei Matsuri Festival last summer.

I wish to extend special thanks to His Worship, Burnaby Mayor Mike Hurley, and members of Burnaby City
Council, for taking time out of their busy schedules to attend many of our key events. At the invitation of the
City of Burnaby, we are working on an initiative to mark the 60th anniversary of the recognition of Burnaby and
Kushiro as sister cities.

I would like to acknowledge the kind and unwavering support of the Consulate General of Japan in
Vancouver. In particular, I wish to thank Consul General Takahashi and Deputy Consul General Okagaki,
who, like their predecessors, have generously lent their valuable time to attend our many events, and have
helped to enhance the visibility of not only our Centre but also other important stakeholder groups within our
Community.

Thank You – And a Request for Your Assistance
Nikkei Centre’s continued success is directly attributable to – and most certainly dependent on - your support, in
the form of: (a) financial donations and testamentary bequests; (b) donations of your valuable time for our many
programs and initiatives that require volunteers; and (c) simply showing up at our many events and inviting your
friends and families to attend as well.

As I expect all of you will have seen, Nikkei Place has recently launched the Nikkei Next fundraising campaign
to fund critical infrastructure upgrades, the reimagining of shared spaces, and the creation of new immersive
cultural experiences. In addition to allowing the Centre to complete much-needed building repairs and
improvements, such as roof and HVAC system upgrades, the Nikkei Next campaign seeks to enable us to
revitalize Hayashi Hall, our Resource Centre Library and our outdoor gardens. Additionally, we are seeking funds
to transform our Tatami Room into an Immersive Cultural Space that will authentically reflect Japanese tradition
and aesthetics, and offer a serene and educational space for cultural appreciation.

I would once again personally like to thank you all for your support and continued interest in Nikkei Centre.



Yoroshiku onegaishimasu.

Heritage Hub




This year marked the launch of the Heritage Hub project, a multi-year initiative supported by the Japanese
Canadian Legacies Society under the Heritage Preservation Pillar. Designed to preserve and amplify the history of
Japanese Canadians, the project focuses on building a dynamic digital platform for the Nikkei National Museum
& Cultural Centre that ensures long-term accessibility, sustainability, and community engagement.

At the heart of the project are four key objectives: digital preservation, accessibility, community engagement,
and education. By digitizing and safeguarding historical documents, photographs, and multimedia materials,
the Heritage Hub will ensure these invaluable resources are preserved for future generations. The creation of
a user-friendly online portal will make this content widely accessible to researchers, students, families, and the
public. The project also emphasizes active community involvement and the integration of educational resources
to deepen awareness and understanding of Japanese Canadian history.

To support this ambitious undertaking, Ryoshin Solutions has been contracted to lead project management
through a phased approach, ensuring the initiative stays on time and on budget. We are pleased to be working
with Ryan Yada, principal of Ryoshin Solutions, whose unique combination of expertise in project management,
technology systems, team leadership, and digital strategy—along with his personal connection as a yonsei Japanese
Canadian and past service on the Nikkei Centre board—brings invaluable insight and dedication to the project.

The project is currently progressing through Phase 1: Research & Evaluation. This phase focuses on establishing
the initial project charter, governance, and defining the vision and purpose; extensive requirements gathering
with input from varied stakeholders; strategy and policy revisions; and evaluating potential solutions. We will enter
Phase 2 of Implementation in the 2025–26 fiscal year, and look forward to sharing more updates and information
about the project in the coming year.

Nikkei Matsuri 2024			
The 12th annual Nikkei Matsuri, held from August 30
to September 1, celebrated Japanese culture with
a vibrant mix of traditional and contemporary arts,
food, and music. The festival featured a mikoshi
parade, cultural demonstrations, bon-odori dancing,
yatai food, and a popular Otakara Market, alongside
special guests from Japan—kimono designer Kiwa
Anraku and sanshin master Masaki Yogi. Welcoming
dignitaries and supported by 250+ volunteers and
500+ participants, the festival successfully fostered
community engagement and cultural sharing, with
proceeds benefiting the NNMCC’s programs.
