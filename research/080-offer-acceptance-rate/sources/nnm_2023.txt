Executive Director’s Message
一華開五葉

Ikka goyō ni hiraku hiraku

One blossom opens into five petals ~ zen phrase

This year, activity at the NNMCC fully returned without PHO restrictions, and our hard-working staff supported
by invaluable volunteers have achieved much, as outlined in this year’s annual report, while managing new and
some unexpected challenges. It was a year to look inwards at our records management, storage, and spaces,
and continue to conduct significant repairs and maintenance on the facility, such as our aging HVAC system.

One such challenge occurred over the Christmas holiday season, when on December 24th, following severe
cold and ice, a roof leak on the Northwest side of the building caused flood damage to the staff office and our
museum repository. Thanks to custodian Rainier Dalisay, the leak was discovered early, and staff banded together
to save our precious collection of objects, artwork, and archival materials. Although impacted by flood - staff
were displaced during office restoration, the gallery was closed temporarily to utilize the space for recovery, and
materials were restored through conservation work - we suffered no loss of the museum collection, and minimal
damages. This was truly a test of our team’s ability, and a demonstration of our commitment to preserving
Japanese Canadian history.

In the coming year, we look forward to embarking on the NNMCC’s Japanese Canadian Heritage Hub project
through agreement with the Japanese Canadian Legacies Society, under the Heritage Pillar of BC Redress. By
the end of January of 2028, our project will result in a new website featuring a Heritage Portal of consolidated
resources, and most importantly, an enhanced online Collections Database providing worldwide access to our
museum collection and that of our database partner organizations.

For myself, this was a year of learning and expanding my understanding of the history at Nikkei Place, while
covering the Interim ED role at the Nikkei Place Foundation, following the departure of Naomi Kawamura, and
prior to the arrival of Keiko Funahashi as the new NPF Executive Director. It has been a pleasure to work more
closely alongside Executive Director, Jay Haraga of Nikkei Seniors Health Care and Housing Society, as we
continue to work towards improved communications across Nikkei Place. I look forward to sharing more of our
Nikkei Place story with you all.

The NNMCC is one organization at Nikkei Place with many components - a museum, cultural centre, and garden;
a gathering place for community; a place to celebrate arts and heritage; a place to learn and share; a place to
grow; a place of remembrance. To operate, deliver on our mission, and reach our goals year after year, it takes a
collaborative team, and this year was no different. I’d like to thank all the staff for your dedication to your work at
the NNMCC, our board directors and numerous volunteers for your commitment to our success, and our patrons
- donors, sponsors, visitors, and attendees of our varied programming – for your unwavering support. Thank you
all for being a part of the Nikkei National Museum & Cultural Centre.




Karah Goshinmon Foster
Executive Director

President’s Report
As President of the Nikkei National Museum & Cultural Centre, I am proud of our many accomplishments
during the past year.

Notably, the reach of our extensive educational and archival resources has continued to expand beyond
academics researching the wartime experience of Japanese Canadians and students at local schools – all
of whom remain very important to us – to leading employers who have called upon us to assist them in
supporting their Equity, Diversity and Inclusion initiatives. The Centre’s mission - to honour, preserve and
share the history and heritage of Japanese Canadians and Japanese culture in Canada – remains more
important than ever in the face of growing intolerance and a disturbing rise in incidents fueled by hatred.

Our Community is relatively small but is widely dispersed across Canada, and, as a west coast based national
institution, we remain committed to expanding our digital footprint and virtual access to the Museum’s
collection. Under the leadership of Director-Curator Sherri Kajiwara, Collections Manager Lisa Uyeda and
museum Staff are working diligently to enhance online access to digital and physical resources curated to
encourage research and engagement with Japanese Canadian history and heritage.

Important groundwork for this initiative was laid over a decade ago. Since 2010, the Centre and the Japanese
Canadian Cultural Centre have worked together on grant-funded digitization projects. This collaboration
has resulted in the publication of The National Japanese Canadian Digitization Strategy which is available for
download on our website (https://centre.nikkeiplace.org/resources/national-japanese-canadian-digitization-
strategy/). The strategy was funded by the Government of Canada Digital Access to Heritage – Museum
Assistance Program, and advanced with invaluable support from the National Association of Japanese
Canadians, the Nikkei Internment Memorial Centre, the Kamloops Japanese Canadian Association Museum &
Archives, and the Sunshine Valley Tashme Museum.

 Since the lifting of COVID pandemic related restrictions in March 2022, the Centre has once again become
an important hub for Japanese culture in the Lower Mainland. Favourites such as the Nikkei Craft Fair,
Mochitsuki, Japanese Book Sales, and Farmers’ Markets remain as popular as ever, and the 11th annual Nikkei
Matsuri Festival more than lived up to its reputation as one of Canada’s largest authentic and family-friendly
Japanese festivals with over 9,000 attendees. Special thanks once again to Louise Akuzawa, Chair of the
Nikkei Matsuri Organization Committee, for her leadership in organizing this very successful event, and to the
other members of the Committee – namely, Cheryl Honkawa, Ron Kruschen, and Wakako Morris.

On May 6, 2023, we held a commemorative event at the Centre to honour Mitsuo and Emmie Hayashi.
Although Mits and Emmie are no longer with us, their legacy remains. To honour them, the Centre’s Event
Hall has been renamed the Mitsuo and Emmie Hayashi Hall. We were honoured to have in attendance special
guest Kayoko Ihara, Mits’s niece from Japan, as well as Consul General Kohei Maruyama.

Governance
Our current Board of Directors held its first meeting immediately following our last annual general meeting
and has continued to meet bi-monthly. The Executive Committee has continued to meet monthly. Meetings
of the Finance Committee, the Fundraising Committee and the Governance Committee have been held on an
as-required basis.

The change from a monthly to a bi-monthly meeting schedule for the Board of Directors has helped facilitate

I would like to acknowledge and thank the members of our Board for their continued attention and support.

I would also like to acknowledge the many contributions of Karah Goshinmon Foster, our dedicated and

Acknowledgement and Appreciation
As I have said many times before, Nikkei Centre could not function without the generous and valuable
contributions of the NNMCC Auxiliary, our many volunteers, and the tireless efforts of our talented and
dedicated Staff. On behalf of the Board of Directors, I commend and thank you all for your continued
support.

Thank You – And a Request for Your Assistance
Nikkei Centre’s continued success is directly attributable to – and most certainly dependent on - your support,
in the form of: (a) financial donations and testamentary bequests; (b) donations of your valuable time for our
many programs and initiatives that require volunteers; and (c) simply showing up at our many events and
inviting your friends and families to attend as well.

I would once again personally like to thank you all for your support and continued interest in Nikkei Centre.

Yoroshiku onegaishimasu.




Herbert I. Ono

Online Programs & Projects
NNMCC and the Vancouver Japanese Language School
& Japanese Hall (VJLS&JH) launched Okaeri おかえり:
Return from Exile – All Paths Lead Home, an oral
history video project in February during Heritage Week
on NNMCC’s Youtube channel. Okaeri おかえり features
21 captivating interviews with Japanese Canadians who
experienced the hardships of the Second World War.
The project sheds light on the individual journeys of the
nisei or second-generation Japanese Canadians who
endured internment in Canada, as well as the kika-nisei,
who were also nisei but were uprooted to Japan and
subsequently returned to Canada in the 1950s. The 18
videos have been viewed 101 to 478 times.

The Timeless Allure of Washi, an online talk presented
by Nancy Jacobi of the Japanese Paper Place (Toronto)
in October in conjunction with the Washi exhibit
attracted over 300 registrants. Up to 120 viewers joined
live from around the world: Pakistan, England, Israel,
Malaysia, Canada, and USA.
